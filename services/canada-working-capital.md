# Financing the gap between paying out and getting paid.

Working capital financing can help a Canadian business cover an operating need before the related cash arrives. The important question is how that cash will return. Start with the payment dates for payroll, inventory, suppliers, and existing debt, then compare them with realistic customer collection dates.

I review financing requests online across [all Canadian provinces and territories](/where-i-operate/). Share the amount, currency, location, and reason for the gap so we can assess the available routes.

## A loan or a line of credit?

A **working capital loan** can suit a defined funding need with a repayment plan. A **line of credit** allows borrowing and repayment within the facility's terms and approved limit. A recurring seasonal need and a one-time project deserve different questions, even if the requested amount is identical.

BDC describes its [working capital loan](https://www.bdc.ca/en/financing/working-capital-loan) as a way to support business cash flow and growth projects. Its product terms apply only to its own offers. Banks and other providers have their own requirements; a product name alone does not tell you its price, security, or repayment terms.

## Compare the full payment obligation

Before deciding, put each written offer into the same comparison:

- **Cash received:** the amount available after any deducted fees.
- **Payment amount and frequency:** daily, weekly, monthly, or another schedule.
- **Total repayment and fees:** include establishment, administration, renewal, and other stated charges.
- **Security and guarantees:** identify the assets and people included in the agreement.
- **Early repayment:** ask what changes if you repay before the scheduled end.
- **Renewal or redraw:** confirm whether future access is committed or requires another approval.
- **Currency:** compare financing obligations with the currency of the cash that will repay them.

A factor rate or flat fee is not the same as an annual interest rate. Ask the provider to explain the cost calculation and use the written agreement as the reference. Our [working capital calculator](/working-capital-calculator.html) can compare simplified offers; its results are estimates, and the assumptions need to match the actual offer.

## Test a slower month before accepting an offer

Make a simple weekly cash forecast with customer receipts, operating expenses, existing obligations, and the proposed payment. Then move a major customer receipt to a later week and check the remaining cash. This is a planning exercise, not a prediction: it helps reveal whether a payment schedule leaves room for ordinary delays.

If the need is a long-lived machine, also compare [equipment financing](/services/canada-equipment-financing/). If unpaid invoices drive the gap, ask whether an invoice-based structure is suitable. Borrowing cannot by itself resolve a persistent mismatch between revenue and operating costs.

## What to prepare

Bring recent business bank statements, current financial information, a list of existing financing payments, and an explanation of the cash cycle. An invoice aging report or inventory plan can clarify the request when relevant. The exact documents depend on the product and provider.
